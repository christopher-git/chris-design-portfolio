Redesign2022

Platform Redesign: Navigation, Load Cards & Control Tower

An extensible navigation and load-card system built to support multimodal tracking, control-tower views, and cross-team collaboration.

Design Leadership · Interaction Design · Systems Thinking

The scope

FourKites' core tracking platform had grown mode by mode — Truck Load, LTL, Ocean, Rail, Parcel, Air — without a unifying navigation or card system to hold it together. The brief: design an extensible pattern that supports every mode, multiple languages, white-labeling, and parent-child page nesting, while maximizing screen real estate and letting customers move fluidly between a Loads-List card view and a map view.

Load cards that respect the shipment's phase

Every load card looked the same regardless of where a shipment actually was in its journey — even though a load moves through three distinct phases (pre-pickup, in-transit, at-stop) that each call for different data and different customer actions. Showing an ETA prediction before a load has even been picked up, for instance, is actively misleading. The redesign made cards phase-aware and mode-flexible, and introduced clearer visual language for which data points were system-inferred versus customer-provided — closing a trust gap for customers without a machine-learning background trying to interpret a confidence score.

Search, filters, and a team workspace

The existing filter panel consumed a third of the screen while still feeling noisy and hard to use. I redesigned search and discovery around how customers actually think about their freight — many retail customers don't think in "loads" at all, they think in POs and SKUs — and introduced the concept of a shareable team workspace built from saved filters and tags, giving teams a shared place to prioritize and collaborate on the loads that matter to them.

Control tower and international modes

The executive control tower dashboard only tracked by load, with no way to view by PO. I redesigned it to support both, alongside a dedicated international view — bookings, containers, bills of lading, and vessel-level tracking — so customers moving freight through ocean and rail could follow one shipment end-to-end across mode changes without losing context.

Why it mattered

This wasn't a single feature — it was the connective tissue underneath FourKites' entire tracking experience: navigation, cards, filters, and the executive view all needed to work as one coherent system across every mode the platform supported, not as five separately-designed experiences bolted together.
