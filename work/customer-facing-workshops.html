Customer Facing Workshops

A repeatable discovery workshop format for co-creating solutions directly with customers, built and run across dozens of Fortune 500 accounts.

Workshop Design · Facilitation · Discovery Research · 5 minute read

Overview

Over the course of my career I've led numerous customer workshops, bringing together diverse groups of customers from different companies and roles, each presenting its own set of challenges that demanded thorough preparation. My responsibilities spanned the full lifecycle: planning, setting clear objectives, selecting participants, crafting agendas, choosing the right workshop activities, preparing materials, facilitating the room, prioritizing what came out of it, validating outcomes, documenting the process, and following up on commitments and feedback. Central to all of it was a razor-sharp focus on facilitation, open communication, and confronting real design challenges head-on. Two workshops from that series are detailed below.

Workshop 1: Reducing Critical Order Risk & Improving Order Performance

Location: Fairmont Millennium Park, Chicago
Date: September 28, 2022
Type: Workshop, Discovery

"Reducing Critical Order Risk & Improve Order Performance with Real-Time Order Visibility."
Workshop objective

Cross-functional stakeholders, planners, customer service representatives, supply chain managers, rely on real-time order visibility to make informed decisions that mitigate order risk, including OTIF fines and penalties. But that information sits buried across multiple systems (OMS, TMS, WMS, YMS) or siloed within teams, requiring heavy manual effort and cross-department collaboration just to analyze and act on it.

The workshop set out to understand how these things get done today, surface the current challenges, and evaluate the tool stack and process needed to design connected solutions that centralize and automate the work.

Need statement: Ability to easily identify the reason for order delays and shortages, and ways to mitigate them collaboratively with suppliers, customers, and carriers, plus the ability to spot trends behind the biggest OTIF fines, like frequent order changes from specific customers, to find easy wins.

Key takeaways:

Post-workshop: One of the clearest takeaways was the need to show order changes directly in the UI, so users know where and when a given event was updated. That fed straight into quick prototyping right after the session.

Workshop 2: AI Recommendations for Supply Chain Risk & Exception Management

Location: Hyatt Regency Chicago
Date: September 6, 2023, 8:00 AM – 12:00 PM
Facilitator: Christopher Gnanadurai · Co-facilitator: Sawyer Smith · Scribe: Jyoti Prakash Mishra

Goal: Gain a deep understanding of supply chain risk and exception management and our customers' user journeys, then ideate innovative AI solutions that address their needs.

Objectives:

  • Gather insights on current risk and exception management processes, identifying pain points, challenges, and opportunities for improvement
  • Identify the challenges and benefits of adopting AI-based approaches to risk and exception management, technical and business alike
  • Brainstorm ways to use FourKites' integrated ecosystem and real-time updates to coordinate risk and exception management with AI-based recommendations
  • Turn those recommendations into actionable automation that improves efficiency and risk management

Participants spanned 28 people from Ace Hardware, Armada Supply Chain Solutions, Cardinal Health, Clearwater Paper, Dow Chemical, HNI, Penske, Schreiber Foods, Unilever, James Hardie, Archlynk, CHS Inc., Frito-Lay, Novelis, and Pepsi Logistics.

Prototypes: A customer's order often depends on inbound shipments or products already sitting in the yard, so those inbound orders need prioritization based on revenue or customer relationship. Three use cases came out of the session:

  • Automated risk assessment and disruption alerts: as a supply chain manager, get proactively notified of potential shipment disruptions and risks so I can act fast to mitigate impact.
  • Priority-based shipment recommendations: weigh value, customer history, inbound delay, and cost together so planners can make the best call on order promising and fulfillment.
  • Delayed-inbound impact for customer service: surface which outbound orders are affected by a delayed inbound shipment, in transit or in the yard, so reps can promise a better ETA.

Why this format works

These workshops aren't a one-off research exercise. They're a repeatable format I've run across a wide range of enterprise accounts to pressure-test problems directly with the people living them, turning a room of competitors and peers into a working session that produces a shipped direction, not just a slide deck.
