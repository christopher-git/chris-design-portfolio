Customer Facing Workshops

A repeatable discovery workshop format for co-creating solutions directly with customers, built and run across dozens of Fortune 500 accounts.

Workshop Design · Facilitation · Discovery Research

Overview

Over the course of my career I've led numerous customer workshops, bringing together diverse groups of customers from different companies and roles — each presenting its own set of challenges that demanded thorough preparation. My responsibilities spanned the full lifecycle: planning, setting clear objectives, selecting participants, crafting agendas, choosing the right workshop activities, preparing materials, facilitating the room, prioritizing what came out of it, validating outcomes, documenting the process, and following up on commitments and feedback. Central to all of it was a razor-sharp focus on facilitation, open communication, and confronting real design challenges head-on rather than working around them.

Example: Reducing Critical Order Risk with Real-Time Order Visibility

One representative workshop, held in Chicago with 13 participants from Coca-Cola Consolidated, Kimberly-Clark, Smithfield, Sprouts Farmers Market, Dollar Tree, Clearwater Paper, Schreiber Foods, Odyssey Logistics, and Canfor, tackled a shared problem: order-risk information buried across OMS, TMS, WMS, and YMS systems, forcing manual, cross-department effort just to understand and act on order exceptions.

Structure

The three-hour session ran a deliberate arc: an icebreaker and stage-setting on the size of the problem, a 60-minute "Ask the Experts" post-up activity surfacing how things get done today and where the pain is, affinity mapping to cluster the raw input, a vote to narrow to the top ideas, a 5-Whys discussion to get past symptoms to root cause, and a closing decision on what to focus on next.

Outcome

The workshop converged on a clear need statement — the ability to quickly surface the reason behind an order delay or shortage, and to act on it collaboratively with suppliers, customers, and carriers, including spotting trends (like a customer with frequent order changes) that were driving the bulk of OTIF fines. The concrete takeaway that shipped out of the session: surface order changes directly in the product UI, so operators know exactly where and when an event was updated instead of hunting for it — validated immediately afterward with a quick prototype.

Why this format works

These workshops aren't a one-off research exercise — they're a repeatable format I've run across a wide range of enterprise accounts to pressure-test problems directly with the people living them, turning a room of competitors and peers into a working session that produces a shipped direction, not just a slide deck.
