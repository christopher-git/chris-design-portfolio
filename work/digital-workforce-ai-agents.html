Digital Workforce: AI Agents for Supply Chain Ops

Design strategy for four AI agents (Tracy, Sam, Alan, and Polly) that cut manual workload 50% and lifted enterprise adoption to 85% in six months, now grown into a six-agent digital workforce running at Fortune 500 scale.

Director of User Experience · Strategy to Launch · 6 minute read

Overview

As Director of User Experience, I led the design strategy for FourKites' AI-powered digital workforce, a suite of agents (Tracy, Sam, Alan, and Polly) that transform supply chain operations. In just 6 months, we achieved a 50% reduction in manual workload, a 20% improvement in tracking quality, and an 85% enterprise adoption rate.

Role: Director of User Experience
Timeline: 6 months (Strategy to Launch)
Team: 4 designers, 2 researchers
Business Impact: $15M+ customer value, 3x increase in enterprise deal size

That launch turned out to be the foundation, not the finish line. The digital workforce I helped design and ship has since grown to six named agents in production, executing 2.5M+ actions a month and covering 152 outcomes across five supply chain domains. The rest of this case study covers the four agents I led design for directly, how the platform they belong to has evolved since, and the results at both scales.

The challenge

Supply chain teams were spending 15-20 hours weekly on repetitive manual tasks: document collection, appointment scheduling, carrier coordination. Our enterprise customers couldn't scale operations without proportionally increasing headcount.

Key Insight: Through customer research, I discovered that 87% of supply chain inefficiencies stemmed from communication gaps that could be augmented by AI, not replaced by it.

Strategic Vision:

  • Elevate humans to strategic work, not replace them
  • Create AI agents that feel like trusted team members
  • Design for varying technological capabilities across the ecosystem

My role & contributions

Strategic Leadership

  • Defined the human-AI collaboration model: AI handles repetitive, high-volume tasks while humans focus on exceptions, decisions, and relationships.
  • Advocated for trust, transparency, and configurability as guiding principles for agent design.

UX Design Direction

  • Designed scalable workflows that balanced automation with human oversight.
  • Created configurable UI frameworks so enterprises could adapt tone, SLAs, and escalation rules to their culture.
  • Developed auditing and monitoring dashboards that built confidence in automation.

Cross-Functional Collaboration

  • Partnered with AI engineers, logistics SMEs, and product leaders to align technical capabilities with user needs.
  • Facilitated workshops with enterprise customers (Unilever, Smithfield Foods, Arrow Electronics) to test and refine AI agent workflows.

Meet the digital workforce

Four agents, each built for a specific point of failure in manual supply chain coordination.

Tracy

Carrier Operations · Track & Trace Specialist

  • Automates shipment visibility and carrier compliance through multi-channel outreach: email, WhatsApp, voice, and SMS.
  • Reconciles ML-predicted ETAs against carrier signals, confirms milestones, and escalates exceptions on her own.
  • Impact: resolves 75% of exceptions autonomously today; improved tracking quality 20% in the first 4 weeks; saved 2–3 hours/day per manager.
  • UX focus: bi-directional carrier communication, configurable workflows, transparency dashboards.

Sam

Supplier Operations · Supplier Collaboration Expert

  • Parses supplier email and PDF documents across formats, auto-creates shipments, links them to purchase orders, and flags exceptions on incomplete data.
  • Impact: 70% reduction in manual document processing at 95%+ extraction accuracy; onboarding time cut from months to days.
  • UX focus: conversational interfaces for suppliers, error prevention through real-time validation, AI explainability.

Alan

Facility Operations · Appointment Scheduling Assistant

  • ETA-driven dock appointment automation: creates, reschedules, and confirms appointments as shipment status changes, integrating directly with carrier and yard systems.
  • Impact: piloted at US Cold Storage to an 87% automation success rate and 96% scheduling accuracy; at production scale, 28% improvement in dock appointment adherence and 35% reduction in driver wait times.
  • UX focus: multi-channel orchestration, intelligent prioritization, proactive exception handling. Read the full AI Agent Alan case study.

Polly

Compliance & Documentation · Document Compliance Manager

  • Runs a persistent, multi-channel chase for proof-of-delivery documents, then validates and files them automatically.
  • Impact: zero-touch POD collection; reduced manual effort by 15–20 hours/week; improved compliance rates, including automated customs documentation.
  • UX focus: trust-building workflows, escalation transparency, flexible integrations (email, APIs, SFTP).

The team has grown

Two more agents have joined since this project shipped, extending the same design principles (trust, transparency, configurability) into new domains I didn't design directly, but that carry the model forward.

Cassie

Customer Operations

  • Triages customer email and chat with full shipment context, then answers WISMO ("Where Is My Order") questions with carrier-grade ETAs.
  • Escalates to a human with the full conversation history attached when a question needs one.
  • Impact: customer response time cut from 90+ minutes to under 30 seconds.

Sophie

Workflow Development · AI Developer Agent

  • An agent that builds agents: compiles a plain-English business requirement into an Agent Operating Procedure, then assigns the work to an existing agent or stands up a new one.
  • Positioning: production workflows in days, not months, with no code and no consulting engagement required.

Three ways intelligence reaches the team

The trust and configurability principles I set for Tracy, Sam, Alan, and Polly eventually generalized into a platform-wide model for how any user gets intelligence out of the system, not just from an agent executing a task, but from asking a question or simply opening the app.

  • Ask (Conversational Intelligence): ask any question about the supply chain in plain language and get back data, visualizations, and recommendations grounded in FourKites' network graph, with a one-click path to turn that question into a standing workflow for Tracy to monitor.
  • See (Gen UI): the interface generates a custom view per person, per role, automatically. A VP sees revenue at risk, a coordinator sees today's exceptions by severity, a dock supervisor sees the next four hours. Same platform, same moment, three views, no configuration.
  • Receive (Intelligence Delivery): proactive intelligence delivered into the tools teams already use: scorecards, supplier reports, disruption alerts, and connectors into PowerBI, Tableau, Snowflake, and Databricks.
