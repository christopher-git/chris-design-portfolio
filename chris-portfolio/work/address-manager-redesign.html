Address Manager: Geofence Audit & Redesign

A usage audit and redesign of FourKites' Address Manager, driven by geofence-recommendation performance and real customer time-on-task data.

UX Audit · Interaction Design · Data-Driven Redesign

Starting from data, not assumptions

Address Manager lets FourKites customers manage and maintain every stop address in their network — pickup, delivery, and yard facilities — and define the geofences used to detect arrivals and departures. Rather than redesigning from intuition, I started with a UX audit of real usage: customers were spending an average of 9 minutes 13 seconds a day on the Address home page, and I pulled the ten highest-traffic accounts (Unilever, Dow Chemical, Averitt Express, Penske, Niagara Bottling, and others) to see exactly which features they touched most and where they got stuck.

The geofence problem

Geofences — the virtual boundary FourKites uses to detect a truck's arrival or departure at a stop — were being manually drawn and frequently misconfigured, which degraded tracking accuracy across the network. The redesign introduced a recommended-geofence feature: suggestions based on a customer's own geofence performance history and on better-performing geofences already in use at the same physical location elsewhere in the FourKites network.

Goals grounded in a baseline

Every design decision was tied to a measurable target set against the audit's baseline: cut average time spent managing addresses from 9.75 to 8.8 minutes per month, and lift adoption of recommended geofences from a 24% baseline to a 70% goal across shippers with recommendations available.

Impact

By replacing manual geofence drawing with data-backed recommendations and simplifying the card, list, and detail views around the features customers actually used, the redesign directly targeted the two levers that mattered most: less time spent on a task customers didn't want to be doing, and higher-quality geofences that improve tracking accuracy for every downstream customer relying on that data.
